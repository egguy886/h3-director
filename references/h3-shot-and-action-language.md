# H3 Shot and Action Language

## Design for 4–15 seconds

Use one primary dramatic turn per generation. A 15-second module usually supports one continuous shot or two to four purposeful shots. A cut must reveal new information, space, state, viewpoint, or time; use camera motion for a small distance or angle change.

Do not allocate time by adjectives. Allocate it by visible states and transitions.

## Action causality

Write fragile actions as an ordered chain:

`initial state → trigger → force/decision → movement path → contact/change → recovery/reaction → endpoint`

Name the owner of each motion:

- subject motion;
- prop/object motion;
- environmental motion;
- camera motion.

This separation prevents body movement from becoming camera roll and prevents wind, fire, or waves from moving in contradictory directions.

## Weight, falling, and rescue

For gravity-dependent action, establish:

- support point and center of mass;
- direction of force and travel;
- what catches, slips, bends, or bears weight;
- how clothing, hair, water, debris, and breath respond;
- a readable end state.

For a hanging person, show one secure or failing handhold, shoulders pulled upward, torso and legs hanging below, and clothing dragged downward. Do not describe only “falling dramatically.”

For rescue contact, separate approach, first contact, grip closure, load transfer, and pull or failure. Give the camera coverage only to the phases the clip can afford.

## Transformation

Anchor transformation to one body occupying one location. Describe which surface features replace which, when limb support changes, and the single final anatomy. Keep the camera controlled until the new silhouette is legible. The previous and new forms must not coexist unless coexistence is explicitly part of the story.

## Explosion, fire, weather, and water

Show cause and consequence. An explosion has an origin, pressure direction, debris path, body response, and decay. Fire has a fuel root, upward convection, shared wind direction, smoke, and exposure limits. Waves have travel direction, impact, runoff, and body/cloth response.

Use positive physical instructions first. Add a short prohibition only for a known repeated failure that the positive description cannot disambiguate.

## Camera language

Use H3's official camera vocabulary naturally inside each shot. When useful, specify motion type, amplitude, and speed. Also specify:

- start composition;
- subject relationship maintained during the move;
- screen direction;
- final composition or information revealed;
- whether the horizon and vertical axes remain stable.

For 9:16 narrative work, compose for the vertical frame: foreground/midground/background depth, stable head-up orientation, readable hands and faces, and enough lower-frame space for gravity or footwork. Do not use “diagonal body” as a substitute for dynamic blocking when it risks camera roll.

## Blocking and geography

Establish where each person stands relative to doors, rails, furniture, vehicles, or landscape anchors. Preserve left/right movement across cuts unless crossing the axis is a deliberate, visible event. If a character leaves frame and reappears, state the path or cut relationship.

## Dialogue and lip sync

- Keep lines short enough for the available shot time and performance pauses.
- Show the speaking mouth unobstructed for requested lip sync.
- Put delivery, accent, timbre, pace, breath, and action outside `<d>`.
- Put only `[Language]` and exact words inside `<d>`.
- Let a line land before a major cut unless H3's `<scenetrans>` continuity rule is intentionally used.
- Use silence, breath, or gaze when the body can carry the beat better than another line.

## Light and exposure

Describe source, direction, color, intensity relationship, and interaction with the subject. Preserve faces and important contact points within readable exposure. When fire and moonlight coexist, assign each a stable side or depth role and avoid asking both to dominate the face.

## Sound

Place synchronized dialogue and shot-specific sound cues in the shot description. Summarize ambience, physical sounds, and non-verbal human sounds in `overall_soundscape`. Put only audience-only score in `non_diegetic_music`. For an episodic chain without a continuous score plan, use `N/A` for music and carry continuity through environment, breath, impacts, and room tone.
