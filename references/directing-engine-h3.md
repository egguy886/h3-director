# Directing Engine for H3

## One intention

State what the audience should experience in one sentence. Make composition, lens feel, camera movement, blocking, performance, light, sound, and cut rhythm serve that same intention.

If a craft choice cannot answer “why here?”, remove or re-aim it.

## Scene-function starting points

| Function | Starting setup | Performance carrier | Refuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intimate dialogue | eye-level medium close-up, minimal move, motivated soft source, sparse sound | withheld look, interrupted task, hand betrays calm | roaming coverage |
| Power confrontation | opposed angles/heights, controlled distance change, split motivated light | stillness versus one loss of control | neutral symmetry |
| Reveal | conceal first, then disclose with camera or blocking | recognition behavior, not object spectacle | showing everything at once |
| Decision | isolate the chooser and finish on one committing action | a gesture with clear before/after | dialogue alone carrying the choice |
| Pursuit/action | disciplined screen direction, tracking tied to the objective, visible effort and recovery | weight, breath, near-miss, consequence | stacked moves with unreadable geography |
| Transformation | controlled camera, one body, one cause, readable replacement and aftermath | response to the change | duplicate forms or spectacle without continuity |
| Emotional low | distance, stillness, negative space, restrained sound | contained micro-action | score-led sentiment |

Adapt these setups to the project's established voice. They are starting points, not templates.

## Performance

- Convert emotion into one filmable behavior per beat.
- Give the focal performer an objective, obstacle, and tactic.
- Use contradiction between line and body for subtext.
- Keep secondary performers on persistent, low-complexity micro-motion.
- Choose one performance register—restrained realism, heightened action, or deliberate stylization—and keep it consistent.

## Camera

- Shot size controls intimacy and context.
- Height and angle encode power and sympathy.
- Lens feel controls psychological space: wider feels exposed or pressured; longer feels isolated or compressed.
- Movement expresses audience impulse: push toward recognition, pull away to reveal cost, track to share pursuit, hold still to force attention.
- A move must begin for a reason and finish on the action or information it exists to reveal.

## Lighting and color

- Name believable sources: moon, fire, window, practical lamp, screen, streetlight, doorway.
- Use contrast ratio to control exposure versus concealment.
- Use direction deliberately: frontal protects, side reveals conflict, back/rim separates, top judges, under-light unsettles.
- Keep a restricted project palette. Change light only when the narrative turn motivates it.
- Describe how moving subjects pass through stable light rather than making light randomly follow every face.

## Sound

Build sound in layers: environment, physical action, body, dialogue, then optional audience-only score. Choose one decisive synchronized cue for the turn. Let density rise with threat and thin for intimacy. In connected AI clips, prefer scene sound and silence over independently generated music unless a continuous-score plan exists.

## Project voice

Choose a functional voice rather than imitating a named director:

- observational naturalist;
- composed classicist;
- kinetic visceral;
- expressive stylist;
- intimate minimalist;
- graphic formalist.

Store the voice in sequence state. A deliberate break in voice should mark a major turn, not random variation.

## Coherence test

Before compilation, check:

1. Can the intention be stated in one sentence?
2. Does every craft choice serve it?
3. Is the performance observable?
4. Are camera and light motivated?
5. Does the setup match the project voice?
6. Is the complexity safe for identity, hands, contact, text, and lip sync?
